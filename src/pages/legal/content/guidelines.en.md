# Community Guidelines and Moderation

Version 0.2 (draft) · Last updated: [DATE OF PUBLICATION]

Messenger should be a place where people can talk without fear. These guidelines are part of the
[Terms of Use](#/terms). They also explain, as the EU Digital Services Act requires, how reports and moderation
work.

## 1. What is not allowed

- **Illegal content**, for example threats, incitement to hatred, depictions of child sexual abuse, terrorist
  content, fraud, or content that violates copyright or someone's privacy.
- **Spam**: unwanted mass messages, advertising, chain messages, automated sending.
- **Scams and fraud**: tricking people out of money, accounts or data, phishing links.
- **Harassment and bullying**: insults, threats, stalking, repeated unwanted contact, sharing private
  information about others.
- **Impersonation**: pretending to be another person, organisation or Messenger itself.
- **Inappropriate profiles**: names, pictures, bios or backgrounds that are sexual, violent, hateful or
  illegal.
- **Packs that break these rules**: sticker and emoji packs with violence, sexual content, hate speech or
  material you have no right to use.
- **Harm to minors**: any content or contact that sexualises, exploits or endangers children.

## 2. How to report

- **In the app:** open the person's profile and choose **Report**, or choose **Report pack** on a sticker or
  emoji pack. You can add details (up to 500 characters) and block the person at the same time. The reported
  person never learns who reported them. You can send up to 20 reports a day.
- **A message or a group:** choose **Report** in the message menu, or **Report group** in the group's info.
  A message report keeps a copy of the message and up to 5 messages before and after it, as you see them, so
  that a moderator can understand the context. A group report keeps the group's name, description and picture.
  Moderators see only this copy, never the rest of the chat. The copy is kept for 6 months.
- After a decision, the app tells you whether action was taken on your report, but not which action.
- **By email**, especially for illegal content or if you are not a Messenger user:
  myron.ilchenko@gmail.com. Please include:
  1. what the content is and where it is (username, group, pack link, and the time of the message),
  2. why you believe it is illegal or breaks these guidelines,
  3. your name and email address (not required for reports of child sexual abuse material),
  4. a statement that you believe in good faith that your report is accurate and complete.

We confirm email reports and tell you what we decided.

## 3. How moderation works

### 3.1 Automatic account status

Reports from users are added up into a score. A report counts more when the reported person started the
contact (for example messaged or called you first), less when you only share a chat, and least when you have
no chat in common. Reports from very new accounts, from restricted accounts, from people who report very many
others, and sudden waves of reports from strangers count less or not at all. Reports count fully for 30 days,
half until 90 days, then no longer.

Depending on the score, the account status changes **automatically**:

| Status | What happens |
|---|---|
| Good standing | Nothing is restricted. |
| Warned | A notice is shown; nothing is restricted yet. |
| Limited | New chats, calls and group invitations only with contacts; at most 5 new chats a day; new chats go to the other person's Requests. |
| Restricted | Additionally: no new groups, no uploads, no forwarding of files, no profile changes, no new sticker or emoji packs, not shown in search. |
| Suspended | Read-only: you can read your chats, delete your account and appeal. Suspension also needs reports from at least 8 different people on at least 3 different days. |

The status goes down again by itself: when reports get older, and by one level after 30 days without new
reports or changes.

Messages are not read or scanned for moderation.

### 3.2 Review by a person

A small moderation team, appointed by the operator and bound to confidentiality, works in a separate
moderation console. A moderator:

- checks every automatic **restricted** or **suspended** status and every pack hidden after reports, and
  confirms, lowers or lifts it, normally within 72 hours;
- checks reports about messages and groups and can remove a message that breaks these guidelines;
- can set an account's status with a reason, dismiss reports that were unfounded, hide or restore a pack, and
  in serious cases delete an account.

A status set by a person is not lowered by itself over time and cannot be appealed automatically in the app;
ask for a review instead (section 3.4). New reports made after the decision can still raise it.

### 3.3 What you are told

When your status changes, the app shows a notice. The **Account status** page in your profile shows your
status since when, exactly which actions are restricted, and when an appeal is possible. A decision by a person
is marked "Reviewed by a person" and shows the reason and an explanation from "Messenger moderation", never a
moderator's name. A message removed by a moderator shows as "Removed by moderation" for everyone in the chat,
and its sender sees the reason.

### 3.4 Appeals and review requests

- **Appeal in the app:** you can appeal an automatic status once every 30 days. Your status then drops one
  level at once for a 14-day probation. Without a new report during the probation, the lower level stays. A new
  report ends the probation, restores the previous status and blocks appeals for 90 days.
- **Ask for a review by a person:** on the **Account status** page, choose **Request a review by a person** and explain
  in up to 500 characters why you think the decision is wrong. You can have one open request at a time and send
  up to 3 in 30 days. A moderator checks it, can lift or change the status, and the result appears on the
  Account status page. You can also write to myron.ilchenko@gmail.com with your username.
- You can also go to court, and in the EU you can turn to a certified out-of-court dispute settlement body.

### 3.5 Action on content reported by email

For reports by email we check the content and the context and decide in a timely, careful and objective way,
normally within 72 hours. We may remove content, hide a pack, restrict, suspend or delete an account, and in
serious cases inform the authorities, in particular when there is a threat to someone's life or safety. We tell
the affected person what we did, why, and how to challenge it, unless the law forbids it, and we tell the
sender of the report what we decided.
